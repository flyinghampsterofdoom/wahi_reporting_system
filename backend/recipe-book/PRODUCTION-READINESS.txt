WAHI-RECIPE-01 — PHASE 5 PRODUCTION READINESS ASSESSMENT

Recommendation: ready for controlled local management acceptance; NOT ready for production release.
This is a requirements/runbook assessment, not authorization to deploy, migrate, provision accounts,
configure infrastructure, purchase services, or integrate excluded development histories.

1. Private photograph storage — release blocker
The current PrivateLocalRecipeMedia adapter deliberately rejects hosted use. Hosted startup in
backend/deploy/runtime.js does not construct any recipeMedia adapter. Setting a local media-root
environment variable is not a production upload solution. An approved persistent private-storage
adapter and its hosted runtime wiring must be implemented, reviewed and tested before release.
Owner must choose the storage location/provider, durable volume or object-store architecture,
budget, regional requirements and operational ownership. Nothing was purchased or configured here.

The adapter must preserve store(bytes,mimeType)/display(meta) semantics, immutable random object
keys and recipe/media IDs. Upload validation currently lives in the local adapter: its equivalent
must be retained or extracted into shared server-side validation before another adapter is accepted.
Maintain <=8 MiB decoded size, JPEG/PNG/WebP actual-format matching, complete decode, <=24M pixels,
single-frame restriction, EXIF orientation normalization, metadata-stripped display WebP inside
1400x1600 without cropping/enlargement, and content integrity verification. Original uploads remain
private and are never returned by static/public routes. Use restricted service credentials, encrypted
transport/at-rest protection, private ACLs and key/credential rotation with controlled access.

Protect delivery through the existing authenticated API: both wells may access only approved images
of active current publications; managers may access draft/history images according to current role
checks. Preserve same-origin/CSRF on uploads, no-store/nosniff delivery and role revalidation. Public
URLs, public buckets, shared API/image browser caches or weaker signed-URL policies are not acceptable
shortcuts. Historical publication IDs retain their exact photo references. Replace/remove changes
references rather than overwriting objects. No automated media deletion/GC is implemented. Any future
retention/GC policy requires reference audits and approval; never delete a published reference.

2. Backup and restoration — release blocker
Owner must define recovery-point/recovery-time objectives, retention, storage/access controls,
encryption/key escrow, restore ownership, monitoring and a periodic restore-rehearsal schedule.
Back up service entries/drafts/immutable versions/events/media, the complete migration ledger, source
identity dependencies and account/authorization configuration, together with every referenced
original, derivative and media manifest/integrity record. Back up the broader production database
using its existing operational policy; this feature is not permission to replace other domains.

Use a consistent DB snapshot and a media snapshot covering all objects referenced by that snapshot.
With immutable append-only media, taking the DB snapshot before copying all referenced media can
preserve consistency; prove reference coverage, do not assume it. Do not omit old publication photos
or only save visible thumbnails. Verify snapshot/object checksums and missing-reference reports;
restrict originals and credentials in backups. Restore to an isolated environment first, with email,
Toast calls and scheduled jobs disabled and production account/session credentials invalidated or
isolated. Verify schema checksums, immutable history, source links, photo bytes, current publication,
draft isolation, both station roles and protected domains before any authorized recovery cutover.

Local evidence: recipe-acceptance.test.js exercises the review tool's private pg_dump/media checkpoint,
restores into a second disposable database, validates schema/history/drafts, verifies original SHA256
and derivative bytes, and checks publication/photo access with both fixture wells. This proves the
local format/procedure, not a production provider's encryption, durability or restore SLA.

3. Database migration — release blocker pending approval/preflight
Phase 5 adds NO migration. Recipe Book history has 031_recipe_book_foundation.sql and
032_recipe_builder.sql. Every earlier approved migration filename/checksum is unchanged. Reserved
021 and 024–030 remain excluded. Production's last reviewed baseline was 0d23a5f during earlier
recovery; no production database or deployment state was accessed or reverified during Phase 5.

Before release, obtain an authorized read-only migration-ledger/deployment reconciliation against the
exact release commit, inspect independent history numbering and constraints, take a verified backup,
and rehearse fresh/upgrade graphs on an isolated production-like clone. Inspect users role constraints,
station-name protection and excluded soft-deletion behavior explicitly. Do not copy original dirty
migrations, fill gaps, renumber deployed files or import unrelated histories. If 032 is independently
allocated elsewhere, stop for owner resolution; use a later additive reconciliation after review.

Run the explicitly approved migration job only after preflight and backup/rollback decisions. Startup
only checks the exact manifest; it must not silently apply schema changes or provision station users.
Verify the installed ledger/checksums and unchanged costing/inventory/waste/labor data after migration.
Retain migration scripts in every rollback-compatible build; never drop service history or attempt a
destructive SQL downgrade. Review DDL locks and maintenance timing using a production-like clone.

4. Station accounts and devices — separate authorization required
No existing or production station account was created, changed or provisioned. Automated tests use
only ephemeral fixture identities in disposable databases. Before production, owner must explicitly
authorize well.one and well.two, assign friendly Well One/Well Two labels and RECIPE_ONLY access,
complete secure password/setup handling and device custody/session policies through existing named
account controls. Do not promote them to Staff/Manager or share management credentials. No production
fixture password, account hash, session or test database dump may be reused.

Verify independent device sessions, direct Book landing and restoration, recipe-only navigation,
server default-deny for Builder/admin/costing/inventory/labor/waste/Toast APIs and draft images,
logout/account switch, access revocation and offline expiration. Maximum offline retention remains
12h capped by the existing authenticated 8h session. Management previews/drafts/staged photos must
never enter the account-scoped published cache. Revocations clear content when reconnected; already
cached permitted content can persist only until the existing bounded lease expires. Confirm the
owner accepts those offline/device tradeoffs before deployment.

5. Production configuration and build — release blocker
Use a separately approved exact release commit/artifact and existing HTTPS origin, WAHI_ENVIRONMENT,
WAHI_PUBLIC_ORIGIN, WAHI_DATABASE_URL, WAHI_AUTH_USERS_JSON and integration-key configuration controls.
Do not print or copy secret values. The existing hosted runtime validates its allowed database target;
do not weaken that guard to make acceptance fixtures work. Fixtures never load hosted configuration.
Add approved media credentials/runtime wiring without conflating them with costing or Toast settings.

Build with the package engines requirement (Node >=22), pinned lockfile/Sharp native dependencies and
the target host's OS/architecture; run production-like build/startup/media smoke tests. Local evidence
uses Node 25.8.1, PostgreSQL 16 and Chrome; hosted-platform compatibility was not asserted here. Confirm
filesystem/object durability, size limits, service permissions, CSP/security headers, server time and
no debug/development banners. Configure operational metrics for upload failures, missing objects,
storage capacity, authorized delivery errors, source conflicts and backup/restore failures without
logging private originals, passwords or session tokens. Exercise staging before owner release approval.

6. Rollback and recovery — compatibility build required
Database history and media references are retained; roll back application behavior using a reviewed
build compatible with the installed 031/032 manifest, station authorization and published document
contract. Bare pre-Recipe-Book builds will fail the strict migration ledger check after new migrations.
Phase 5 additionally widens service quantity precision to 24 fractional digits to retain observed
19-digit source decimals exactly. A63767d's 12-digit draft validator can reject newly imported documents;
therefore it is not an unconditional rollback target after such documents are saved. Prepare and test
a compatibility build that retains the expanded validator and schema while withdrawing new UI behavior
if required. No feature-disable/rollback mechanism was added or executed under this work order.

For content mistakes, Admin recovery copies an old immutable version into draft. Preview and explicitly
republish to change the current reference; recovery does not alter menu classification or automatically
publish. Restore historical photos by their retained original media IDs, not overwriting image objects.
Archive/restore is service-only and does not alter costing. If media is unavailable, preserve DB/history
and show the placeholder; restore approved media from a verified backup. Approval revocation remains
possible without mutating media identity/content. Any full database recovery/cutover requires separate
owner authorization and protected-domain reconciliation.

7. Content and operational acceptance — outstanding
The five genuine source imports are verified, but actual preparation, garnish and glassware remain
unsupplied; actual photographs are also unavailable. Missing photographs alone do not block publication.
Management must author and approve the service content using acceptance/WORKFLOW.txt, then complete
all five cocktail checks at both wells. Automated completed-recipe station/recovery checks use clearly
separate synthetic controls and do not constitute real house-content sign-off. Do not deploy or claim
production readiness until these content and infrastructure gates are resolved and explicitly accepted.
